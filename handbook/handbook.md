## Section 1: Company Overview

### 1.1 About MediCore Bangladesh Ltd.

MediCore Bangladesh Ltd. ("MediCore" or "the Company") was incorporated in 2004 under the Companies Act, 1994, as a private limited company. The Company manufactures, markets and distributes prescription medicines for the domestic market. As of the effective date of this handbook, MediCore employs approximately 800 permanent and probationary employees across its head office, manufacturing plant and field offices.

### 1.2 Locations

| Location | Function |
|---|---|
| **Corporate Head Office, Tejgaon I/A, Dhaka** | Executive management, HR, Finance, Supply Chain, Marketing, Regulatory Affairs, IT |
| **Manufacturing Plant, Gazipur** | Production, Quality Control, Quality Assurance, Warehousing |
| **Chattogram Field Office, Agrabad** | Sales and distribution for Chattogram and Cox's Bazar regions |
| **Sylhet Field Office, Zindabazar** | Sales and distribution for Sylhet Division |
| **Rajshahi Field Office, Shaheb Bazar** | Sales and distribution for Rajshahi and Rangpur regions |

Dhaka Division sales operations are managed directly from the Corporate Head Office.

### 1.3 Products and Therapeutic Areas

MediCore's portfolio comprises over 140 registered products (brands and strengths), approved by the Directorate General of Drug Administration (DGDA), across the following therapeutic areas:

1.3.1 Anti-infectives (oral and injectable antibiotics)
1.3.2 Gastrointestinal (proton pump inhibitors, antacids, antiemetics)
1.3.3 Cardiovascular and diabetes care (antihypertensives, statins, oral hypoglycaemics)
1.3.4 Respiratory (bronchodilators, antihistamines, cough preparations)
1.3.5 Analgesics and anti-inflammatories
1.3.6 Vitamins, minerals and nutritional supplements

All products are manufactured under current Good Manufacturing Practice (cGMP) guidelines as prescribed by the DGDA and the World Health Organization.

### 1.4 Mission Statement

> *To improve the health of the people of Bangladesh by delivering safe, effective and affordable medicines of uncompromising quality, and by partnering responsibly with the medical community we serve.*

### 1.5 Core Values

1.5.1 **Patient First** — Every decision is made with patient safety in mind.
1.5.2 **Integrity** — We promote our products ethically and honestly, in line with DGDA regulations and the Company's Code of Ethical Promotion.
1.5.3 **Quality** — We do not compromise on manufacturing or documentation standards.
1.5.4 **Accountability** — We own our results and our records.
1.5.5 **Respect** — We treat colleagues, doctors, chemists and partners with dignity.

### 1.6 Employee Grades

For the purposes of this handbook, all employees are classified into one of the following grades. Where a policy differs by grade, the applicable grade is stated.

| Grade | Category | Typical Designations | Work Base |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Grade A** | Officers, Managers and above | Officer, Senior Officer, Assistant Manager, Manager, Senior Manager, Head of Department | Office-based |
| **Grade B** | Field Force | Medical Representative (MR), Senior Medical Representative (SMR), Field Executive (FE) | Field-based |
| **Grade C** | Support Staff | Driver, Office Assistant, Messenger, Support Staff | Office / field support |

Area Managers, Regional Sales Managers and the National Sales Manager are classified as Grade A but supervise Grade B employees.

### 1.7 Applicability of this Handbook

1.7.1 This handbook applies to all permanent and probationary employees of MediCore Bangladesh Ltd.
1.7.2 Contractual, casual and third-party outsourced staff are governed by their respective contracts.
1.7.3 This handbook is to be read alongside the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006 (as amended) and the Bangladesh Labour Rules, 2015. Where any provision of this handbook offers a lesser benefit than the law, the law shall prevail.
1.7.4 The Company reserves the right to amend this handbook. Amendments will be communicated via HR circular and the HR intranet, and shall take effect from the date stated in the circular.

## Section 2: Attendance and Working Hours

### 2.1 Working Week

2.1.1 The Company's standard working week is **Sunday to Thursday**.
2.1.2 Friday and Saturday are weekly holidays, except where an employee is rostered for plant operations, inventory audit or a declared business requirement.
2.1.3 Government-declared public holidays shall be observed as per the annual holiday list issued by HR in December of each year.

### 2.2 Office Hours — Grade A and Grade C

2.2.1 Office hours for Grade A and Grade C employees are **9:00 AM to 6:00 PM**, Sunday to Thursday.
2.2.2 This includes a one-hour break for lunch and prayer between 1:00 PM and 2:00 PM.
2.2.3 Attendance is recorded through the biometric attendance system at the point of entry. Manual registers are maintained only at locations where the biometric system is temporarily non-functional, and must be countersigned by the location's Administration Officer.
2.2.4 An employee is deemed **late** if the first biometric punch is recorded after **9:15 AM**.
2.2.5 Grade C drivers assigned to senior management may be required to report earlier or depart later as per duty roster. Such hours are governed by the Driver Duty Roster issued by the Administration Department and overtime is compensated as per Section 2.6.

### 2.3 Attendance — Grade B Field Force

Grade B employees do not report to an office on a daily basis and **are not covered by the biometric attendance system or the office hours in Section 2.2**. Their attendance is governed exclusively by this sub-section.

2.3.1 **Morning Call-In:** Each Grade B employee must log attendance by calling his or her direct supervisor (Area Manager, or Senior Medical Representative where designated as team lead) **no later than 8:30 AM** on each working day.
2.3.2 During the call-in, the employee must confirm (a) the territory to be covered that day and (b) the planned doctor and chemist calls as per the approved monthly tour plan.
2.3.3 The supervisor must record the call-in in the Field Force Attendance Log (FFAL) on the Sales Force Automation (SFA) system by **9:00 AM**.
2.3.4 **Return Report:** Each Grade B employee must submit the Daily Call Report (DCR) through the SFA mobile application **no later than 6:30 PM** on the same working day. The DCR must list every doctor visited, every chemist visited, samples distributed and orders booked.
2.3.5 A call-in made after 8:30 AM is recorded as a **late**.
2.3.6 A working day on which **either** the morning call-in is not made **or** the DCR is not submitted by 6:30 PM shall be recorded as **absent**, unless the Area Manager approves an exception in writing on the SFA system within 24 hours, citing the reason (e.g. network failure, accident, medical emergency).
2.3.7 Area Managers shall conduct random field verification (joint field work or surprise visits) of each MR at least twice per month. Discrepancies between the DCR and actual field activity shall be treated as falsification of records under Section 8.6.
2.3.8 Grade B attendance records (FFAL and DCR) form the basis for the calculation of Field Allowance under Section 4.

### 2.4 Late Attendance Policy (All Grades)

The following applies to lates recorded under Section 2.2.4 (Grade A and C) and Section 2.3.5 (Grade B), counted per calendar month:

| Number of Lates in a Calendar Month | Consequence |
|---|---|
| 1 to 3 | Recorded; no action |
| More than 3 (i.e. 4th and 5th late) | **Written warning** issued by HR, copied to the line manager |
| More than 5 (i.e. 6th late onwards) | **Deduction of one (1) day's basic salary**, in addition to the written warning |

2.4.1 Late counts reset on the first day of each calendar month.
2.4.2 One day's salary is calculated as monthly basic salary divided by 30.
2.4.3 Three written warnings for lateness within any rolling 12-month period shall be escalated to the disciplinary process under Section 8.7.

### 2.5 Absence Without Leave

2.5.1 Any absence not covered by an approved leave application (Section 3) shall be recorded as Absence Without Leave (AWL) and is unpaid.
2.5.2 Absence without leave for more than 10 consecutive days shall be treated in accordance with Section 27(3A) of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006.

### 2.6 Overtime

2.6.1 Grade C employees are entitled to overtime at twice the ordinary basic rate for hours worked beyond 8 hours per day or 48 hours per week, in accordance with Section 108 of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006.
2.6.2 Overtime must be pre-approved by the Department Head on Form OT-01.
2.6.3 Grade A and Grade B employees are not eligible for overtime payment.

## Section 3: Leave Policy

### 3.1 Leave Year

3.1.1 The leave year runs from **1 January to 31 December**.
3.1.2 All leave is applied for and approved through the HRIS leave module. Paper application (Form LV-01) is accepted only where HRIS access is unavailable.

### 3.2 Annual (Earned) Leave

3.2.1 Annual leave entitlement is as follows:

| Grade | Annual Leave (working days per year) |
|---|---|
| Grade A | 20 |
| Grade B | 18 |
| Grade C | 15 |

3.2.2 Annual leave **accrues only after completion of one (1) year of continuous service**. No annual leave may be availed during the first year of service.
3.2.3 Annual leave must be applied for at least **7 calendar days in advance** and approved by the line manager. Leave of more than 5 consecutive working days also requires the approval of the Department Head.
3.2.4 For Grade B employees, annual leave may not be taken during the last 5 working days of a quarter (sales closing period) except with written approval of the Regional Sales Manager.
3.2.5 Unused annual leave of up to 10 days may be carried forward to the following leave year. Any balance in excess of 10 days shall lapse on 31 December.
3.2.6 Encashment of annual leave is permissible only at the time of separation, calculated on the last drawn basic salary.

### 3.3 Sick Leave

3.3.1 All employees, regardless of grade, are entitled to **14 days of sick leave** per leave year on full pay, in accordance with Section 116 of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006.
3.3.2 Sick leave of **more than 2 consecutive days** must be supported by a medical certificate issued by a registered medical practitioner (BMDC-registered).
3.3.3 The medical certificate must be submitted to HR, through the line manager, within 3 working days of resuming duty.
3.3.4 Unused sick leave cannot be carried forward or encashed.
3.3.5 The Company reserves the right to require an examination by the Company's panel physician for repeated or extended sick leave.

### 3.4 Casual Leave

3.4.1 All employees, regardless of grade, are entitled to **10 days of casual leave** per leave year on full pay, in accordance with Section 115 of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006.
3.4.2 Casual leave must be applied for to the immediate supervisor **on the same day**, before the start of working hours. For Grade B employees, this means before the 8:30 AM call-in deadline under Section 2.3.1.
3.4.3 Casual leave may not be taken for more than 3 consecutive days and may not be combined with annual leave.
3.4.4 Unused casual leave cannot be carried forward or encashed.

### 3.5 Maternity Leave

3.5.1 Female employees who have completed at least 6 months of service are entitled to **16 weeks (112 days)** of paid maternity leave — 8 weeks before and 8 weeks after the expected date of delivery — in accordance with Chapter IV of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006.
3.5.2 Notice must be given to HR in writing, along with a medical certificate confirming the expected delivery date, at least 8 weeks prior to the commencement of leave.

### 3.6 Other Leave

3.6.1 **Festival Holidays:** 11 days per year as per the Company holiday list, in addition to the leave above.
3.6.2 **Bereavement Leave:** 3 working days on the death of a spouse, parent, child or sibling.
3.6.3 **Leave Without Pay:** May be granted for up to 30 days at the discretion of the Department Head and HR, only after all other eligible leave has been exhausted.

### 3.7 Leave Summary

| Leave Type | Grade A | Grade B | Grade C | Notice / Documentation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Annual Leave | 20 | 18 | 15 | 7 days advance; after 1 year of service |
| Sick Leave | 14 | 14 | 14 | Medical certificate if > 2 consecutive days |
| Casual Leave | 10 | 10 | 10 | Same-day application to supervisor |
| Maternity Leave | 112 days | 112 days | 112 days | 8 weeks' notice with medical certificate |

## Section 4: Field Allowance

### 4.1 Purpose

The Field Allowance (FA) compensates field-based employees for daily incidental expenses incurred while performing doctor and chemist calls within their assigned territory, including local conveyance, meals and mobile data.

### 4.2 Eligibility

4.2.1 Field Allowance is payable to **Grade B employees only**.
4.2.2 Grade A and Grade C employees are not eligible for Field Allowance, including Area Managers and drivers who accompany field visits. Their expenses, where applicable, are claimed under Section 5.

### 4.3 Daily Rates

| Designation (Grade B) | Daily Field Allowance |
|---|---|
| Senior Medical Representative (SMR) | BDT 650 per day |
| Medical Representative (MR) | BDT 500 per day |
| Field Executive (FE) | BDT 400 per day |

4.3.1 The allowance is payable only for working days on which the employee is recorded as present under Section 2.3.
4.3.2 No allowance is payable for weekly holidays, public holidays, any type of leave, or absent days.
4.3.3 Where an employee is promoted mid-month, the revised rate applies from the effective date stated in the promotion letter.

**Illustration:** An MR recorded present for 20 working days in a month is entitled to 20 × BDT 500 = BDT 10,000 in Field Allowance for that month.

### 4.4 Out-Station Work

4.4.1 Where a Grade B employee is required to work outside the assigned territory and stay overnight, the Field Allowance is not payable for those days. Instead, travel and accommodation are claimed under Section 5.

### 4.5 Claim Procedure

4.5.1 Field Allowance is claimed monthly on **Form FA-01 (Monthly Field Allowance Claim)**.
4.5.2 The completed FA-01, together with the system-generated monthly DCR summary, must be submitted to the **Area Manager by the 5th of the following month**.
4.5.3 The Area Manager shall verify the claim against the FFAL and DCR records and forward it to the Regional Sales Manager by the 8th.
4.5.4 The Regional Sales Manager shall approve and forward the claim to Sales Administration, which shall submit consolidated claims to Finance by the 12th.
4.5.5 Approved Field Allowance is paid with the following month's salary.
4.5.6 Claims submitted after the 5th will be processed in the next monthly cycle. Claims older than 60 days shall not be accepted.

### 4.6 Minimum Attendance Requirement

4.6.1 **Field Allowance is not payable for any month in which the employee's attendance falls below 80% of the working days in that month.**
4.6.2 For this purpose, "attendance" means days recorded as present under Section 2.3. Approved leave of any type, absent days and days marked absent under Section 2.3.6 are not counted as present.
4.6.3 Where the threshold is not met, the **entire** Field Allowance for that month is forfeited. There is no pro-rata payment.
4.6.4 The 80% threshold is calculated on the number of working days in the relevant calendar month, rounded up to the nearest whole day.

**Illustration:** In a month with 22 working days, 80% equals 17.6 days, rounded up to 18 days. An MR who is present for 17 days receives no Field Allowance for that month.

4.6.5 Exceptions may be granted only by the National Sales Manager and the CPO jointly, in writing, in cases of hospitalisation, natural disaster or a declared emergency.

### 4.7 Misuse

4.7.1 Any Field Allowance claim found to be based on falsified DCRs or attendance records shall be recovered in full from salary and treated as falsification of records under Section 8.6.

## Section 5: Travel and Claims

### 5.1 Scope

This section applies to all employees travelling on official business outside their normal place of work or assigned territory, including out-station field work, training, conferences, plant visits and audits.

### 5.2 Travel Authorisation and Advance

5.2.1 All official travel must be authorised in advance by the line manager on **Form TR-01 (Travel Request and Advance)**.
5.2.2 Form TR-01 must be submitted **at least 48 hours before the scheduled departure**.
5.2.3 Where a cash advance is required, the estimated amount must be stated on TR-01. Advances are released by Finance only against an approved TR-01.
5.2.4 TR-01 forms submitted less than 48 hours before travel will be processed only with written justification approved by the Department Head. No advance will be paid in such cases; expenses must be claimed after return.

### 5.3 Mode of Travel — Bus, Train and Launch

5.3.1 Bus (AC or non-AC), train (any class up to AC chair/berth) and launch (up to single cabin) are the standard modes of official travel.
5.3.2 These modes **do not require any approval beyond the line manager's approval of Form TR-01**.
5.3.3 Fares are reimbursed at actual cost, supported by the original ticket or e-ticket.

### 5.4 Mode of Travel — Air

5.4.1 Domestic air travel is permitted only in exceptional circumstances, such as urgent business requirements, travel to Cox's Bazar or Saidpur where time is critical, or medical emergencies.
5.4.2 **Air travel requires written pre-approval from BOTH the employee's Department Head AND the Human Resources Department.** Approval from one party alone is not sufficient.
5.4.3 Pre-approval must be recorded on Form TR-01 (Section C: Air Travel Approval), signed by both the Department Head and the HR Business Partner, before the ticket is booked.
5.4.4 Air tickets must be booked through the Company's designated travel agent in economy class.
5.4.5 Air fares incurred without the dual pre-approval in Section 5.4.2 will **not be reimbursed**, regardless of business justification provided after the fact.

### 5.5 Accommodation

5.5.1 Accommodation is reimbursed at actual cost **up to BDT 2,500 per night**, supported by an original hotel invoice showing the employee's name, dates of stay and amount.
5.5.2 Any amount above BDT 2,500 per night shall be borne by the employee unless specifically approved in advance by the Department Head on TR-01.
5.5.3 Where a Company guest house is available at the destination, employees are expected to use it. Hotel costs will not be reimbursed where guest house accommodation was available and not used without valid reason.

### 5.6 Local Conveyance and Meals on Out-Station Travel

5.6.1 Local conveyance at the destination (CNG auto-rickshaw, rickshaw, ride-sharing) is reimbursed at actual cost up to BDT 500 per day. Ride-sharing receipts are required; for other conveyance, a self-declaration on the claim form is accepted.
5.6.2 A meal allowance of BDT 600 per day is payable for each full day of out-station travel.

### 5.7 Expense Claim Submission

5.7.1 All travel expenses must be claimed on **Form TR-02 (Travel Expense Claim)**, with original receipts attached, and the advance (if any) adjusted.
5.7.2 **The claim must be submitted within 7 calendar days of the date of return.**
5.7.3 **Claims submitted after 7 days will not be accepted.** No exceptions will be made on grounds of workload or oversight.
5.7.4 Where an advance was taken and no claim is submitted within 7 days, the full advance will be deducted from the employee's next salary.
5.7.5 Approval chain: Line Manager → Department Head → Finance (Accounts Payable). Finance will reimburse approved claims within 10 working days.

### 5.8 Non-Reimbursable Items

5.8.1 The following are not reimbursable: personal entertainment, alcohol, laundry (for stays of less than 5 nights), mini-bar charges, traffic fines, and travel by family members.

## Section 6: Medical Benefits

### 6.1 Purpose

The Company provides an annual medical allowance to assist employees with outpatient medical expenses for themselves.

### 6.2 Annual Medical Allowance

| Grade | Annual Medical Allowance |
|---|---|
| Grade A | BDT 15,000 |
| Grade B | BDT 10,000 |
| Grade C | BDT 6,000 |

6.2.1 The allowance is available per leave year (January–December) and is reimbursed against actual claims up to the stated limit.
6.2.2 Unused allowance does not carry forward and is not encashable.
6.2.3 Employees joining mid-year are entitled to a pro-rata allowance based on the number of full months remaining in the year.

### 6.3 Covered Expenses

The following expenses are reimbursable within the annual limit:

6.3.1 General consultation fees of BMDC-registered doctors.
6.3.2 Prescribed medicines, supported by the prescription. Where a MediCore brand is available for the prescribed molecule, employees are encouraged (but not required) to use it.
6.3.3 Diagnostic tests (pathology, imaging, ECG) prescribed by a registered doctor.
6.3.4 Optical expenses (eye examination, prescription spectacles and lenses), **for Grade A only**, subject to Section 6.4.

### 6.4 Exclusions and Grade Restrictions

6.4.1 **Dental treatment is NOT covered for any grade.** This includes dental consultation, scaling, fillings, extraction, root canal treatment, orthodontics and dentures.
6.4.2 **Optical expenses are covered for Grade A employees only.** Grade B and Grade C employees may not claim optical expenses under any circumstances.
6.4.3 For Grade A, optical claims are limited to one pair of prescription spectacles or lenses per leave year, within the overall BDT 15,000 limit.
6.4.4 The following are also excluded for all grades: cosmetic procedures, non-prescribed supplements, vitamins without prescription, fertility treatment, and in-patient hospitalisation (covered separately under the Group Health Insurance policy, where applicable).

### 6.5 Claim Procedure

6.5.1 Claims are submitted on **Form MED-01 (Medical Reimbursement Claim)**.
6.5.2 Form MED-01 must be accompanied by **original receipts**, the doctor's prescription and, for diagnostic tests, the test requisition. Photocopies or photographs of receipts will not be accepted.
6.5.3 Claims must be submitted to HR within 60 days of the date of the expense.
6.5.4 HR shall verify the claim and forward it to Finance. Approved amounts are paid with the next month's salary.
6.5.5 Grade B employees at field offices shall submit MED-01 through their Area Manager, who will forward it by courier to HR, Dhaka.

## Section 7: Probation and Confirmation

### 7.1 Probation Period

7.1.1 All newly appointed employees, regardless of grade, shall serve a probation period of **six (6) months** from the date of joining.
7.1.2 The probation period is stated in the appointment letter.

### 7.2 Mid-Probation Performance Review

7.2.1 The line manager shall conduct a formal performance review at the **end of Month 4** of probation on **Form PR-04 (Probation Review)**.
7.2.2 The review shall assess job knowledge, quality of work, attendance, conduct and adherence to Company policies. For Grade B employees, the review shall also assess call achievement against tour plan, product knowledge and DCR compliance.
7.2.3 The completed PR-04 must be signed by the employee and submitted to HR within 7 working days of the review.
7.2.4 Where performance concerns are identified, the line manager shall set specific written improvement targets for the remaining probation period.

### 7.3 Confirmation

7.3.1 At the end of Month 6, the line manager shall submit a confirmation recommendation to HR on **Form CF-01 (Confirmation Recommendation)**, endorsed by the Department Head.
7.3.2 Where performance is satisfactory, HR shall issue a **confirmation letter within 30 days of the completion of probation**.
7.3.3 Upon confirmation, the employee becomes a permanent employee and all benefits applicable to permanent employees take effect from the date of confirmation.

### 7.4 Unsatisfactory Probation

Where performance during probation is assessed as unsatisfactory, the Company may, on the recommendation of the line manager and Department Head and with HR approval, take one of the following actions:

7.4.1 **Extend probation by three (3) months**, with written improvement targets. An extension may be granted only once, making the maximum probation period nine (9) months.
7.4.2 **Terminate employment with one (1) month's notice** or one month's basic salary in lieu of notice.

### 7.5 Benefits During Probation

7.5.1 Probationary employees are entitled to sick leave and casual leave on a pro-rata basis.
7.5.2 Probationary employees are not entitled to annual leave (see Section 3.2.2).
7.5.3 Probationary employees are entitled to the medical allowance under Section 6 on a pro-rata basis.

### 7.6 Field Allowance During Probation

7.6.1 **Probationary employees are NOT eligible for Field Allowance under Section 4 during their first three (3) months of service.** This applies to all grades.
7.6.2 Field Allowance eligibility, for those otherwise eligible under Section 4.2, begins from the first day of Month 4 of service.
7.6.3 During Months 1 to 3, newly joined Grade B employees undergo induction training and accompanied field work with the Area Manager or a designated SMR. Out-station expenses incurred during this period are claimed under Section 5.
7.6.4 No Form FA-01 shall be submitted for any day falling within the first three months of service.

### 7.7 Resignation During Probation

7.7.1 A probationary employee who wishes to resign must give 15 days' written notice or forfeit 15 days' basic salary in lieu.

## Section 8: Grievance and Conduct

### 8.1 Purpose

The Company is committed to providing a fair, respectful and safe workplace. This section sets out how employees may raise grievances and the standards of conduct expected of every employee.

### 8.2 Grievance Procedure

An employee who has a work-related concern or complaint shall follow the steps below:

8.2.1 **Step 1 — Verbal Discussion with Line Manager:** The employee should first raise the matter verbally with the immediate line manager, who shall attempt to resolve it informally within 5 working days.

8.2.2 **Step 2 — Written Complaint to HR:** If the matter is not resolved at Step 1, the employee may submit a written complaint to HR on **Form GR-01 (Grievance Submission)** **within 7 days** of the Step 1 discussion. Where the grievance concerns the line manager directly, the employee may skip Step 1 and proceed directly to Step 2.

8.2.3 **Step 3 — HR Investigation:** HR shall acknowledge receipt within 2 working days and complete an investigation **within 14 working days** of receipt of GR-01. The investigation may include interviews with the complainant, the respondent and witnesses, and review of documents. HR shall communicate its findings and decision to the complainant in writing.

8.2.4 **Step 4 — Appeal to the CPO:** If the employee is not satisfied with HR's decision, he or she may submit a written appeal to the **Chief People Officer (CPO)** within 7 days of receiving the HR decision. The CPO's decision, communicated within 10 working days, shall be final within the Company's internal process.

8.2.5 Nothing in this procedure limits an employee's rights under Section 33 of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006.

### 8.3 Confidentiality

8.3.1 The contents of any grievance, and the identity of witnesses, shall be shared **only on a need-to-know basis** with those directly involved in resolving it.
8.3.2 All grievance records shall be held securely by HR and shall not form part of the complainant's personnel file.
8.3.3 Any employee who breaches the confidentiality of a grievance process shall be subject to disciplinary action.

### 8.4 Non-Retaliation

8.4.1 No employee shall suffer any adverse action for raising a grievance in good faith or for participating as a witness. Retaliation is treated as serious misconduct.

### 8.5 Code of Conduct

All employees are expected to act with honesty and professionalism, comply with all applicable laws (including the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 2023 and DGDA promotional guidelines), and protect the Company's reputation in their dealings with doctors, chemists, distributors and the public.

### 8.6 Prohibited Conduct

The following are strictly prohibited and constitute misconduct:

8.6.1 **Falsification of Records:** Including false DCRs, attendance records, Field Allowance claims, travel or medical claims, sample distribution records, and quality or batch documentation.
8.6.2 **Harassment:** Including sexual harassment, bullying, intimidation, or discriminatory behaviour based on gender, religion, ethnicity, disability or any other ground. Complaints of sexual harassment are additionally handled by the Company's Complaints Committee in line with the High Court Division guidelines (2009).
8.6.3 **Conflict of Interest:** Including undisclosed financial interest in a distributor, supplier or chemist; offering inducements to doctors outside the Code of Ethical Promotion; and private sale of Company samples. Employees must declare any potential conflict annually on **Form COI-01**.
8.6.4 **Misuse of Company Assets:** Including personal use of Company vehicles, sale or diversion of physician samples, misuse of Company SIM cards or data packs, and unauthorised use of IT systems.
8.6.5 **Disclosure of Proprietary Information:** Including disclosure of formulations, pricing, sales data, marketing plans, doctor databases or any confidential information to competitors or any unauthorised third party, during or after employment.

### 8.7 Disciplinary Procedure

8.7.1 Disciplinary action shall follow a progressive sequence:

| Stage | Action | Issued By | Validity |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Verbal Warning** (recorded in file note) | Line Manager | 6 months |
| 2 | **Written Warning** | HR, on recommendation of Line Manager | 12 months |
| 3 | **Final Written Warning** | HR, with Department Head approval | 12 months |
| 4 | **Termination** | CPO, with Managing Director approval | — |

8.7.2 Before any action at Stage 2 or above, the employee shall be issued a show-cause notice and given **7 days** to respond in writing, in accordance with Section 24 of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006.
8.7.3 Where misconduct is serious — including proven falsification of records, theft, sexual harassment, or disclosure of proprietary information — the Company may proceed directly to termination following a domestic enquiry, without exhausting the earlier stages.
8.7.4 During an investigation into serious misconduct, the employee may be suspended on subsistence allowance in accordance with Section 24 of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006.
8.7.5 An employee may appeal any disciplinary decision to the CPO within 7 days of receipt, following Section 8.2.4.

### 8.8 Acknowledgement

All employees must sign **Form ACK-01 (Handbook Acknowledgement)** within 7 days of joining, and again whenever a new version of this handbook is issued, confirming that they have read and understood its contents.
